# Button Bunny Sticker Record

This record preserves the source, prompt, references, and review decision for Number Nook's first Special Guest Sticker portrait.

## Selected asset

- Character: Button Bunny
- Collection: Special Guests
- Selected revision: Sticker v1
- Full-resolution source: `src/dev/assets/button-bunny-sticker-v1.png` (1254 × 1254 PNG)
- Art Lab preview: `src/dev/assets/button-bunny-sticker-v1-preview.png` (768 × 768 PNG)
- Production asset: `public/collectibles/button-bunny-sticker.webp` (768 × 768 WebP)
- Classic identity reference: `public/collectibles/button-bunny.svg`

Sticker v1 was selected because the teal plush construction, stitched silhouette, friendly dark eyes, and oversized yellow button remain clear at 96 pixels. The warm reading-nook background connects the portrait to the existing collection without making the Special Guest look like another cat.

## Reference lineage

The generation used four local visual references with distinct roles:

1. Button Bunny's Classic SVG, temporarily rendered to PNG, defined the character identity.
2. Biscuit Sticker v1 supplied the warm indoor lighting and rounded, welcoming presentation.
3. Moonbeam Sticker v1 supplied the polished eye treatment, clean silhouette, and dimensional finish.
4. Cloud Sticker v2 supplied the simple daylight environment and compact mobile readability.

The artwork was generated with the built-in image-generation tool and then passed through the repository's normal preview and WebP optimization pipeline.

## Final generation prompt

> Create a square, full-bleed polished Sticker portrait for the original Number Nook character Button Bunny.
>
> REFERENCE ROLES
>
> - The first reference is the authoritative character-design reference. Preserve Button Bunny's identity: a well-loved teal plush rabbit, two long upright-but-soft plush ears, visible restrained stitching, dark friendly eyes, a tiny pink nose, a gentle smile, and one oversized round yellow button with crossed thread. The yellow button is the unmistakable signature detail.
> - The remaining references are style-only anchors from the same game. Match their polished 2D mobile-game collectible look: clean readable silhouette, appealing large expressive eyes, soft dimensional storybook shading, warm controlled lighting, tidy edges, and a simple illustrated environment.
>
> COMPOSITION
>
> - Exactly one rabbit, centered in a square portrait, looking toward the viewer.
> - Head and upper body fill roughly 70% of the image. Keep both ears and the complete yellow button inside a generous safe margin so the character reads in a 96-pixel card.
> - Make the rabbit visibly a beloved plush toy rather than a living furry rabbit: velvety teal fabric, a few tasteful seam lines and tiny repaired stitches, softly stuffed rounded forms. Keep the texture smooth and stylized, never photorealistic or worn-out.
> - Put the yellow button securely sewn on the upper chest, large and easy to recognize, with four holes and crossed golden-brown thread.
> - Use a cozy Number Nook reading-room setting: softly blurred arched window or bookshelf shapes, warm cream and muted peach light, a small teal cushion, and a subtle golden glow around the button. Background remains simple and subordinate.
> - Give the Special guest a little extra warmth and charm, but no magical explosion, costume, crown, text, or rarity badge.
>
> STYLE
>
> Polished sticker/storybook hybrid; cute, tasteful, family-friendly, softly dimensional, harmonious teal/gold/cream palette; crisp at mobile size; consistent with the supplied cat portraits while clearly being a plush bunny.
>
> AVOID
>
> No cats, no second character, no extra ears or limbs, no loose duplicate buttons, no biscuits, no crescent moon, no written words, no numbers, no UI frame, no logo, no watermark, no transparent background.

## Review notes

- Exactly one rabbit is present with two ears and two arms.
- The portrait reads as a stuffed companion rather than a living rabbit.
- The button has four holes and crossed thread, with no loose duplicate button in the scene.
- The face, ears, seams, and yellow button remain recognizable in the Art Lab's 96-pixel comparison.
- The full portrait is retained for future revisions; only the optimized WebP ships to players.
